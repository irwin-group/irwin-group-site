Kent Irwin

Professor of Physics, of Particle Physics and Astrophysics, and of Photon Science

About

Kent Irwin is Professor of Physics, of Particle Physics and Astrophysics, and of Photon Science at Stanford University and SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, and Director of the Hansen Experimental Physics Laboratory (HEPL). His group uses superconducting sensors and quantum measurement techniques to probe fundamental physics, from axion dark matter searches with DMRadio to new quantum sensors for the radio-frequency band.

As a graduate student at Stanford, Kent invented the superconducting transition-edge sensor (TES) for the Cryogenic Dark Matter Search. He then spent nearly two decades at the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in Boulder, where he became a NIST Fellow and led the Quantum Sensors Group. There, his team developed SQUID multiplexers for reading out large arrays of TES detectors, technologies now used in cosmic microwave background experiments, X-ray spectroscopy, and beyond. He returned to Stanford and SLAC in 2013.

His honors include the APS Joseph F. Keithley Award for Advances in Measurement Science (2007), the Arthur S. Flemming Award (2006), the U.S. Department of Commerce Gold Medal (2012), and the IEEE Council on Superconductivity Award for Continuing and Significant Contributions in Applied Superconductivity (2014).
